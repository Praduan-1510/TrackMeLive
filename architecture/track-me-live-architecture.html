How Track Me Live fits together

Code runs in three places: the sharer's app on iOS or Android, a Firebase backend, and the viewer's browser. The sharer's location flows one way, from the app through Firestore to the browser. During the call, Live Together adds a second, opt-in flow the other way: the viewer's location, back to the app.

Every flow, in words

The same arrows as the map, written out. "App" means both the iOS and Android apps unless a platform is named.

Sharer's location

  1. GPS pings → pings/{id}

    The app writes lat, lng and recorded_at every 3–5 seconds above 20% battery, every 15 seconds from 20% to 10%, and every 60 seconds under 10%, and records the current ping_interval_s. With Approximate on, the phone rounds each point to about 500 m before writing it.

  2. App → last_ping_at

    The app's check-in, not its newest location: refreshed with every ping, and every 30 seconds even without a GPS fix, so losing GPS alone never ends share back.

  3. pings → live map page

    A real-time listener; a new ping reaches both maps within 5 seconds of capture. A person shows as Live while their newest ping is under 3 intervals old (at least 15 seconds), then as "Last seen" with the newest ping's recorded_at. While the sharer is Approximate, both maps show a soft circle 1 km across with no trail, and distances are rounded to 0.5 km: "about 1 km", "under 1 km".

  4. Sharing in the background

    Sharing always starts in the app, with "While using the app" location only. On iOS it continues with the system location indicator and the Live Activity; iOS has no Back to call, so the sharer returns through the system's green call indicator. On Android it runs as a foreground service with the notification "Sharing your location with Mom", with Open and Stop sharing.

Share back (Live Together)

  1. Session controls → share_back_allowed, share_back_requested_at

    Let them share back is a switch on the share screen, on by default. Ask to share, from the viewer's row, works once per call and only after the viewer has opened the link. A session has at most one call, and Share again during that call keeps Ask to share used.

  2. Session → share-back panel

    Only the first browser to open the link (viewer_uid) sees the Share my location card and "Alex asked to see where you are", and only if the sharer allowed share back. Later browsers, such as a forwarded link, can only watch.

  3. Share-back panel → viewerPings/{id} and viewer_sharing

    Off by default; starts when the viewer taps Share my location and the browser allows it. Rules accept these writes only from viewer_uid, while the session is active and before expires_at, with share_back_allowed and call_active true and last_ping_at under 2 minutes old. It pauses while the page is in the background.

  4. viewerPings → Live Together map

    The app listens and shows both markers and the straight-line distance, in the app, the Android mini map and the iOS Live Activity. On the sharer's map the viewer is a crossing-blue disc with the initial of the name the sharer gave, or a person icon; on the viewer's own page it's always a person icon labelled You. A decline is never reported; the row stays "Watching".

Session, call and alerts

  1. CallKit or TelephonyManager → session controls

    A call that starts while the app is open and not already sharing shows the in-app banner. Neither platform reports calls to an app that isn't running, and the app never knows who is on the call. iOS needs no permission. Android uses TelephonyManager with the Phone permission; until it's granted, the audio mode (MODE_IN_CALL) while the app is open, which engineering confirms in a spike. Without the permission, shares use a 30-minute timer and share back is off.

  2. Quick start → share screen

    During a call, the iOS control (Control Center, Lock Screen or Action button) or the Android Quick Settings tile opens the app on the share screen for this call. No extra permission.

  3. Session controls → Cloud Functions

    Start session: creates shareSessions/{sessionId} with status active, started_at, stop_rule (the call, minutes or the 1-hour limit) and stop_at (capped at expires_at, 1 hour away), call_id (the call connected now; none for a manual share), share_back_allowed, precision and the sharer snapshot; plus the sharer-only private/meta. Then the share sheet opens. Only one session runs at a time.

  4. Share sheet → viewer's browser

    Messages, WhatsApp, Mail or Copy link; the app never sends texts itself. If the sheet closes without a choice, the session is deleted before anyone can see it. Copy link counts as sent, and Send link again reopens the sheet with the same link. Share links always open the web page, even when the recipient has the app: the app doesn't claim the /s/ path.

  5. Live map page ↔ Cloud Functions

    Only the page's own script sends the link token, so link previews never count as an open; a preview shows only the sharer's name. If the token matches an active session, the function signs the page in anonymously for that one session, records the first browser as viewer_uid, sets first_viewed_at on the first open and sends the view alert.

  6. Session controls → shareSessions

    call_active is true only while the call in call_id is connected; once that call ends it stays false, and a later call never turns it back on. If the app comes back while the same call is still connected, it sets it again. Stop sets status to stopped with ended_at and end_reason (stopped, call, timer or limit).

  7. shareSessions → live map page

    The page follows status, call_active, share_back_allowed, precision, ping_interval_s and the sharer snapshot taken at the start; it never reads users or private/meta. Reads stay open after the end: "Alex stopped sharing at 9:52 pm" after Stop, otherwise "Sharing ended at 9:52 pm", and "No longer being shared" for a link opened later.

  8. Cloud Functions → APNs and FCM → apps

    Alerts to every phone in users/{userId}/devices: the first open, a call ending while sharing, and sharing expiring. On iOS, APNs also carries Live Activity updates (status and stop time) to the live_activity_token in private/meta; the phone works out the distance from the viewer's latest share-back ping. Five minutes before the limit, the app, the Live Activity and the Android notification show a quiet "Sharing stops at 10:38 pm · 1-hour limit".

  9. Cloud Functions ↔ Firestore

    The call watchdog clears call_active when last_ping_at is more than 2 minutes old. When call_active turns false, a function sets viewer_sharing to false. A scheduled stop ends each session at the earlier of stop_at and expires_at, even if the app was killed, and a call-tied session 10 minutes after the watchdog clears call_active. Pings and viewer pings go 24 hours after the end; session metadata and call logs after 30 days.

Sign-in, profile, maps and hosting

  1. Sign in ↔ Firebase Authentication

    Name and mobile number, confirmed with a 6-digit text code (filled in from Messages where the phone allows). A new code after 30 seconds. The same flow signs back in on a new phone. Viewers get only the anonymous, one-session sign-in.

  2. Profile and mascot → users, devices, callLogs

    The app saves the profile, mascot choice, settings and last_viewer_name; signing in on a new phone restores them, never a session. It registers each phone's push token in devices (deleted at sign-out or when the token stops working), reads the mascots library, and links each call to its session by call_id. Deleting the account stops any session and deletes everything at once.

  3. App and live map page → Maps API

    The Maps API draws the map in the app and on the viewer page; the viewer's browser also looks up the nearest street or area for the text line ("Near Harbor Way · 1.2 km north-east of you"). Nothing is geocoded or stored on the backend. While Alex is approximate, the line names only the area and Open in Maps opens the rounded point.

  4. Firebase Hosting → live map page

    Serves the page itself, the sharer's mascot render and its accessory layer. The apps bundle the same renders; if a render can't load, the marker falls back to the flat SVG, then a plain lamplight disc.
